## 1. Executive Thesis: Navigating the Intrinsic vs. Relative Valuation Gap

Triangulating Infosys Limited across fundamental and market-based methodologies uncovers a pronounced **valuation divergence** around the current share price of **₹1,151**:

| **DCF — Sensitivity Band** | **₹665.54 – ₹1,673.38** | **$-42.2\%$ to $+45.4\%$** | WACC ($11.0\%–18.0\%$) & Terminal $g$ ($3.0\%–7.0\%$) |

### Analytical Synthesis
1. **Why the DCF Screens Conservative ($1.25\text{x}$ P/IV):** The intrinsic model discounts cash flows at a relatively demanding **$12.87\%$ WACC**—driven by a $7.21\%$ risk-free rate and a $7.94\%$ historical Indian equity risk premium—while tapering EBIT growth from $9.50\%$ to $4.00\%$ in perpetuity. Because **$69.9\%$ (₹251,664 Cr)** of Infosys's ₹360,137 Cr operating asset value resides in the terminal value, valuation is highly sensitive to discount rate assumptions. A $187\text{ bps}$ reduction in WACC to $11.00\%$ (at $4.0\%$ terminal growth) lifts intrinsic value to **₹1,084/share**, within $6\%$ of the market price.
2. **Why Relative Multiples Imply Substantial Upside:** Infosys trades at **`9.2x` EV/EBITDA** and **`15.0x` P/E**, representing a $43\%$ and $36\%$ discount to the 10-company peer median (`16.1x` and `23.4x`). While the peer median is skewed upward by high-growth mid-cap ER&D/digital specialists—**Persistent Systems** (`39.4x` EV/EBITDA) and **Coforge** (`30.6x` EV/EBITDA)—Infosys still trades at a discount to every Tier-1 large-cap peer: **Wipro** (`10.7x`), **TCS** (`11.7x`), and **HCLTech** (`14.0x`).
3. **Market Pricing Verdict:** Trading at ₹1,151, the market is pricing Infosys below mid-cap multiples to reflect near-term macro caution ($1\%–2\%$ FY27 constant-currency revenue guidance), while assigning a premium over the strict base-case DCF in recognition of its industry-leading ROIC and ₹13,676 Cr net cash fortress.

## 2. Revenue Trajectory, Deal Pipeline & AI Monetization

* **Structural Scale vs. Cyclical Moderation:** Over the decade from FY17 to FY26, Infosys compounded revenue at **$11.2\%$ annually** (from ₹68,484 Cr to ₹178,650 Cr). Following post-pandemic cloud migration tailwinds that drove $>20\%$ annual growth in FY22–FY23, top-line expansion normalized to **$9.61\%$ in FY26**.
* **Guidance vs. Order Book Tension:** Management tempered FY27 constant-currency revenue growth guidance to **$1.0\%–2.0\%$** (down from $1.5\%–3.0\%$), signaling delayed discretionary client spending. However, quarterly large deal Total Contract Value (TCV) remained robust at **\$5.2 billion** ($62\%$ net new), providing strong medium-term revenue visibility once client budget velocity recovers.
* **Generative AI Scaling:** AI-led transformation and automation services reached **$10.5\%$ of quarterly revenue** (accelerating sequentially from $8.2\%$), positioning Infosys to capture vendor-consolidation and productivity-led enterprise mandates.
* **Econometric Baseline:** A 12-year OLS linear regression (`FY15–FY26`, $R^2 = 96.39\%$, $t\text{-stat} = 16.34$) projects sales reaching **₹184,695 Cr in FY27E** and **₹220,230 Cr by FY30E**. While statistically robust as a long-term trendline, linear models inherently smooth cyclical troughs—justifying our decision to anchor DCF projections on fundamental reinvestment economics rather than mechanical trend extrapolation.

## 3. Margin Architecture & Cost Structure Evolution

* **Gross Margin Compression ($-665\text{ bps}$ over 10 Years):** Gross profit margin contracted from **$34.86\%$ (FY17)** to **$28.21\%$ (FY26)**.
* **Isolating the Cost Driver:** Common-size decomposition proves that direct employee compensation was *not* the culprit—employee benefit costs remained tightly disciplined within a $52.6\%–56.1\%$ corridor ($53.24\%$ in FY26). Instead, **other manufacturing/operating expenses nearly doubled from $9.80\%$ to $18.43\%$ of sales**, reflecting structural shifts toward third-party software/hardware pass-through components in integrated deals, cloud infrastructure expenses, and specialized subcontractor utilization.
* **Operating Discipline Preserves Bottom-Line Margins:** Management offset over half of the gross margin compression through aggressive SG&A optimization (reducing selling & administrative expenses from $6.69\%$ to $3.80\%$ of sales) and tax efficiency. Consequently, FY26 Operating EBITDA margin held resilient at **$23.67\%$** (aligning with management's $20\%–22\%$ EBIT margin guidance), while Net Profit Margin stabilized at **$16.48\%$**.

## 4. DuPont Decomposition: Uncovering the True Drivers of 31.21% ROE

A 3-step DuPont analysis ($\text{ROE} = \text{Net Profit Margin} \times \text{Asset Turnover} \times \text{Equity Multiplier}$) between FY20 and FY26 reveals how Infosys expanded shareholder returns despite margin headwinds:

| DuPont Component | FY20 | FY22 | FY24 | FY26 | 6-Year Shift | Attribution Effect |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Net Profit Margin** ($\text{PAT / Sales}$) | $18.28\%$ | $18.18\%$ | $17.07\%$ | $16.48\%$ | **$-180\text{ bps}$** | **Headwind** (Cost mix shift) |
| **Asset Turnover** ($\text{Sales / Avg. Assets}$) | $1.03\text{x}$ | $1.08\text{x}$ | $1.18\text{x}$ | $1.18\text{x}$ | **$+0.15\text{x}$** | **Tailwind** (Capital-light scale) |
| **Equity Multiplier** ($\text{Avg. Assets / Avg. Equity}$) | $1.35\text{x}$ | $1.48\text{x}$ | $1.59\text{x}$ | $1.60\text{x}$ | **$+0.25\text{x}$** | **Tailwind** (Operating leverage) |
| **Return on Equity (ROE)** | **$25.45\%$** | **$29.15\%$** | **$32.08\%$** | **$31.21\%$** | **$+576\text{ bps}$** | **Net Value Creation** |
| **Return on Assets (ROA)** | **$18.87\%$** | **$19.72\%$** | **$20.13\%$** | **$19.49\%$** | **$+62\text{ bps}$** | **Steady Core Asset Yield** |

### Key Takeaways for Capital Allocation
* **Asset Velocity & Operating Leverage:** Because core asset profitability (ROA) remained remarkably stable near $\sim 19.5\%$, the $+576\text{ bps}$ ROE expansion was engineered through **capital efficiency** (capital turnover nearly doubled from $0.99\text{x}$ in FY17 to $1.92\text{x}$ in FY26) and **balance sheet optimization**.
* **Zero Financial Debt Risk:** Crucially, the rise in the equity multiplier ($1.35\text{x} \rightarrow 1.60\text{x}$) was *not* fueled by financial borrowing. Infosys carries **zero bank debt**; its ₹9,176 Cr in borrowings consists solely of capitalized lease liabilities (`IFRS 16` / `Ind AS 116`). The multiplier expansion reflects healthy growth in non-interest-bearing operating liabilities (accrued expenses, unearned revenue, and trade payables, which grew from ₹21,717 Cr to ₹52,260 Cr) alongside disciplined capital return via consistent dividends and share buybacks.

## 5. Cash Flow Conversion & Working Capital Watchpoints

* **Elite Earnings Quality:** In FY26, Infosys generated **₹33,986 Cr in Operating Cash Flow** ($115.4\%$ of Net Profit) and **₹31,259 Cr in Free Cash Flow** ($106.2\%$ of Net Profit) after ₹2,727 Cr in CapEx. This cash generation comfortably funded **₹18,656 Cr in cash dividends** ($66\%$ payout ratio) without straining liquidity.
* **Working Capital Watchpoint (Receivables):** While overall cash conversion remains superior, **Debtor Days drifted upward from 66 days (FY17) to 72 days (FY26)** as trade receivables expanded from $14.8\%$ to $22.8\%$ of total assets (₹35,234 Cr). Tightening unbilled revenue and client collection cycles represents an immediate ₹2,500–₹3,000 Cr working capital release opportunity.

## 6. Model Limitations & Institutional Refinements (Self-Critique)

To maintain rigorous analytical transparency, the following methodological nuances and areas for further refinement are documented:

1. **Terminal Year Cash Flow Step-Up:** In the DCF bridge, FY32E FCFF (₹38,518 Cr) is stepped up from FY31E at the explicit-period growth rate ($9.50\%$) prior to applying the Gordon Growth perpetuity formula. Applying the $4.00\%$ terminal growth rate directly to FY31E FCFF yields a slightly more conservative intrinsic value of **~₹887/share** ($-3.4\%$ lower).
2. **Non-Operating Income in Base EBIT:** The FY26 base EBIT of ₹40,377 Cr incorporates other income ($\sim 1.7\%$ of sales). Stripping non-operating treasury income out of EBIT (given that ₹22,201 Cr of cash is separately added in the EV-to-Equity bridge) would prevent minor double-counting of cash yields.
3. **Provisional Intrinsic Growth Series:** The 4-year historical series used to derive the median reinvestment rate ($26.20\%$) and intrinsic growth ($9.50\%$) displays high dispersion ($-9.22\%$ to $+18.29\%$) due to working capital swings and unadjusted historical EBIT inputs; normalizing historical NOPAT across a full 10-year cycle would further stabilize growth inputs.
4. **Peer Group Composition & Beta Choice:** Excluding Infosys from the peer summary statistics and isolating Tier-1 large-cap peers (`TCS`, `HCLTech`, `Wipro`) from mid-cap outliers (`Persistent`, `Coforge`) provides a tighter relative valuation band. Additionally, substituting the Blume-adjusted regression beta ($0.92$) for the peer-relevered beta ($0.76$) raises $K_e$ to $\sim 14.5\%$ and lowers intrinsic DCF value.
